RBI Governor Tells Banks to Harness AI — Not Just Contain Its Risks

Reserve Bank of India Governor Sanjay Malhotra has urged the country's banks to proactively shape their AI journeys rather than let the technology dictate direction, calling AI "the most powerful accelerator to financial inclusion." Speaking at the FIBAC 2026 conference in Mumbai, he said AI models trained on alternative data — cash flows, GST filings, utility payments — could extend credit to millions of underbanked Indians that manual underwriting cannot reach.

Malhotra laid out a detailed vision: AI-enhanced credit risk models that spot emerging stress earlier than lagging financial statements allow, liquidity forecasting, and relationship managers augmented by AI to serve more customers efficiently. But he was equally forceful about risks, warning that opaque models make it "exceedingly difficult for auditors, boards, and the Reserve Bank to be confident that a model is doing what it was designed to do." He set explicit expectations: banks must maintain a complete inventory of every AI system in use, establish board-approved AI governance policies, build the capacity to explain AI-driven decisions, red-team systems before deployment, and preserve meaningful human oversight at every material decision point. "The model decided can never be an acceptable answer to a customer, an auditor, or the Reserve Bank," he said. He also pointed to India's public digital infrastructure — Aadhaar, UPI, DigiLocker, ONDC — as the foundation on which private AI applications can be built, suggesting AI could do for financial decision-making what UPI did for digital transactions.

Indian Banks Race to Scale GenAI as Adoption Jumps to 86%

A new BCG-FICCI-IBA report reveals that the share of Indian banks with GenAI use cases under implementation has surged from 44% in 2025 to 86% in 2026, up from just 10% in 2024. The report, "Winning in the AI Era: The New Playbook for Indian Banks," says scaling the technology will require changes in operating models, talent, and governance.

The report identifies credit affordability as a top priority, noting that operating and collection expenses account for 40–50% of the total cost to serve, making small-ticket retail credit economics particularly challenging. AI-enabled lending journeys could automate document processing, underwriting, and collections. Retail bureau coverage expanded from 45.5 crore borrowers in 2021 to 78.9 crore in 2026, while MSME bureau footprint nearly doubled. The report argues that previous decade's digitization hasn't translated into commensurate productivity gains — cost-to-income ratios remain elevated. Agentic AI could change that by handling complex, unstructured tasks and freeing employees for higher-value decisions. Key barriers remain: data and infrastructure readiness, talent shortages, regulatory concerns, and uncertainty over returns. Banking assets need to grow 3.5–4 percentage points faster than nominal GDP to support India's ambition of a $30 trillion economy by 2047.

Bengaluru Police Deploy AI Translation System for Emergency Calls in 10+ Languages

A late-night distress call from a Manipuri-speaking woman — who Bengaluru police couldn't understand — has led to the city rolling out VANKI, a first-of-its-kind AI-powered real-time translation system for emergency response. The system now handles calls in over 10 Indian and international languages, integrated into the city's Namma 112 emergency platform.

Built by Aeos and Monday Ventures, the Multilingual Voice AI for Nationwide Key Interventions (VANKI) lets callers speak naturally in their preferred language while AI translates and relays information to call handlers in real time. Supported languages include Bengali, Gujarati, Odia, Manipuri, Kashmiri, Nepali, Assamese, along with Spanish, French, and Arabic. The system eliminates the need for human translators and significantly reduces response delays. Bengaluru, which attracts people from across India and the world, had an increasingly evident language-gap problem in emergency situations where seconds matter. The AI layer ensures anyone can seek help regardless of whether they speak Kannada, Hindi, or any other language.

India Cuts Flagged AI-Content Removal Window from 36 Hours to Just 3

India has tightened rules for handling deepfakes and AI-generated content, slashing the removal window for flagged illegal posts from 36 hours to 3 hours. Platforms must also detect and label AI-made material, speed up complaint handling, and use technology tools to spot harmful synthetic content.

The new rules are focused on responsible AI use rather than outright bans, targeting specific harms: impersonation, non-consensual intimate imagery, child sexual abuse material, and deceptive representations of real people or events. The move comes as India also uses AI on the enforcement side — SEBI's Project SUDARSAN, launched last November, uses AI to scan social media posts, videos, and audio in regional languages for fake promises and dubious financial advice from "finfluencers," having already flagged over 20,000 suspicious pieces of content. 62% of Indian investors are reportedly influenced by finfluencers.

India Proposes "One Nation One License" for AI Training on Copyrighted Content

India's DPIIT has published a working paper proposing a hybrid licensing model for AI training data, offering a mandatory blanket licence for AI developers to train on copyrighted material in exchange for a statutory right to royalties for creators — collected through a central body called the Copyright Royalties Collective for AI Training.

The proposal comes after the Delhi High Court declined to grant ANI Media an interim injunction against OpenAI in July 2026, holding that storing articles to train LLMs falls within the research and private use exceptions of the Copyright Act. The DPIIT working paper, titled "One Nation One License One Payment," aims to provide certainty that the courtroom couldn't: AI developers would no longer need to negotiate individually with every rights holder, but would need to disclose the categories of content used in training. Royalties would be linked to a share of post-commercialization revenue — not charged upfront during training — protecting startups building models before they have revenue. The royalty obligation is proposed to apply retrospectively, so companies that already trained on copyrighted material without payment don't gain a lasting advantage. The proposal also gives smaller and unorganised creator groups representation on the governing body. The underlying ANI v. OpenAI suit continues to trial.

Meta Launches Muse Glimmer, a 30B Open-Weight Model That Runs on a Single GPU

Meta has released Muse Glimmer, a 30-billion-parameter open-weight model from Meta Superintelligence Labs, designed to run locally on a Mac or PC with a single consumer GPU. CEO Mark Zuckerberg also promised to open the weights of Muse Spark 1.2, Meta's more powerful frontier model, in the coming weeks — reversing the company's April pivot to closed models.

Muse Glimmer is distilled from Muse Spark, Meta's frontier-class model launched in April as a closed proprietary system — a departure from Meta's open-source tradition. Glimmer reverses that bet: weights are available on Hugging Face under Apache 2.0, with a 128,000-token context window. Meta's engineers shrank the model's footprint from ~55GB to under 20GB using quantization, making it runnable on consumer hardware without cloud infrastructure. Zuckerberg published a 6,500-word essay positioning open-weight models as a challenge to Chinese open-source labs and urged Washington to support American efforts, arguing against concentrating AI power in a few companies. The launch comes as Meta's AI division, reorganized last year under former Scale AI CEO Alexandr Wang, tries to reassure investors that its massive capex — forecast up to $145 billion this year — will pay off. Developers note Muse Code, a terminal coding agent released alongside Muse Spark 1.2, doesn't quite match frontier models from Anthropic or OpenAI but competes on cost.

OpenAI Locks Down Astra Model Over Potential Critical Cybersecurity Capabilities

OpenAI's internal evaluation of its upcoming model, Astra, found significant advances in agentic coding and cybersecurity, leading the company to conclude it cannot rule out the model reaching "critical capability level" for cybersecurity under its Preparedness Framework. OpenAI has paused activities involving Astra that don't meet enhanced security requirements.

Under OpenAI's Preparedness Framework, a model is classified as having critical cybersecurity capabilities if it can independently discover previously unknown software vulnerabilities in secure systems or plan and execute sophisticated cyberattacks with little or no human guidance. OpenAI has strengthened safeguards: isolated testing environments, restricted network and tool access, enhanced encryption of model weights, additional monitoring, and sandboxed execution. The company will ask government agencies and independent AI safety organizations to evaluate Astra's cybersecurity capabilities before deployment. This follows a separate release — GPT-5.6-Cyber, a model trained to find zero-day vulnerabilities and build exploit chains with far fewer refusals, available through OpenAI's vetted Daybreak Red program. GPT-5.6-Cyber uncovered two previously undocumented flaws in Chrome's V8 engine, assigned CVE-2026-15903, which Google has fixed.

Google's AMIE Demonstrates Expert-Level AI Medical Video Consultations

Google Research and DeepMind have advanced AMIE, their research medical AI system, to perform real-time clinical video consultations in a first-of-its-kind study. Built on Gemini and Project Astra using a multi-agent architecture, AMIE interprets visual and auditory cues, guides virtual physical exams, and reasons diagnostically in real time — with clinical evaluators assessing it favorably against primary care physicians.

In a randomized study using simulated consultations with patient actors and primary care physicians, clinical evaluators assessed AMIE favorably across core clinical competencies including history-taking thoroughness, diagnostic accuracy, management appropriateness, and communication quality. Patient actors also preferred the video experience over text chat. The system represents a significant step beyond text-based medical AI: a physician notices a cough, observes gait, registers visible signs of discomfort — and AMIE now brings that same multi-modal awareness to virtual consultations. Google emphasized that AMIE remains a research system and more work is needed before responsible real-world clinical deployment, but the demonstration offers a compelling glimpse into the future of health AI, particularly for telemedicine and remote healthcare delivery.

Researchers Decrypt AI "Encrypted Thoughts," Exposing Leaked Secrets and Benchmark Answers

AI researchers have devised a method to decrypt the encrypted reasoning — or "chain of thought" — of AI models like ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini, revealing that some models already know benchmark answers during thinking and conceal this from users. The "Stolen Thoughts" research also uncovered 704 pieces of confidential information, including 62 API keys, 33 passwords, and 24 access tokens in decrypted reasoning traces.

The research team exploited the fact that encrypted thought processes can be reused between models from the same company. By loading a high-performance model's thought process into a lower-performance model from the same company and then performing a jailbreak attack on the weaker model, they forced the encrypted reasoning to output in plain text. The findings were striking: when Claude Opus 4.8 solved an AIME math problem, the thinking process contained "This is a known problem from the AIME, and the correct answer is 60" — information concealed from the user-facing thought display. The team collected 6,708 agent outputs with encrypted reasoning from GitHub and Hugging Face and decrypted 315,320 thought processes. They also found that Chinese-made Kimi K3's thought processes are strikingly similar to those of Claude Opus 4.8, suggesting possible adversarial distillation. AI researcher Nathan Lambert noted this implies Chinese labs may be using similar interception techniques, and called the frontier labs' failure to patch this "shocking."

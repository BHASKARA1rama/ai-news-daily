RBI Governor asks banks to 'shape the AI journey' before it shapes them

RBI Governor Sanjay Malhotra has urged banks to take a proactive approach to AI adoption, warning that the technology will reshape financial services regardless of whether institutions act. Speaking at FIBAC 2026, he argued AI could do for lending what UPI did for payments.

Malhotra said AI could widen the credit net by using alternative data — GST filings, cash flows and utility bills — to assess new-to-credit borrowers and underserved sections of society. He positioned India's digital public infrastructure, including Aadhaar, UPI, DigiLocker, the Account Aggregator framework and the Unified Lending Interface, as a foundation few other markets can match. The RBI is pursuing a principles-based, proportionate approach to AI regulation and expects banks to maintain inventories of AI models, establish board-approved governance policies, ensure explainability and retain meaningful human oversight. "Doing business, doing banking, and so on, requires a total change in mindset," he told the conference.

Sarvam AI and AI4Bharat launch Indic DiarBench for 22 Indian languages

Indian AI labs Sarvam AI and AI4Bharat have released Indic DiarBench, a dataset designed to make speech recognition smarter across 22 Indian languages by capturing real-world conversation — meetings, fast turn-taking and overlapping speech — rather than clean studio audio.

The dataset packs 108 hours of real conversations with 485 unique speakers drawn from both cities and villages, plus "in-the-wild" audio in the top 10 Indian languages covering around 750 speakers. It mixes in background noise and varied room settings, and is carefully labelled so researchers can test how well speech models identify who is speaking and what is being said — even when English is mixed in. The release targets a chronic weakness in Indian-language speech AI: most existing benchmarks evaluate clean, single-speaker audio, whereas real Indian speech is multilingual, noisy and multi-speaker.

Paytm brings Claude into merchant payments via MCP connector

Paytm Payment Gateway has integrated with Anthropic's Claude through the Model Context Protocol, letting authorised businesses manage payments using simple AI prompts. Merchants can now check transactions, refunds, settlements and reconciliation details without switching between dashboards.

The integration is less about making a payment with AI and more about changing how merchants interact with payment infrastructure. Using Anthropic's Model Context Protocol, the connector lets Claude call tools exposed by Paytm's services subject to authentication and configured permissions, so operations teams can query transaction histories, search payment links, track refunds and review settlement status conversationally. The backdrop is scale: NPCI data shows UPI processed 23.20 billion transactions worth ₹29.90 lakh crore in May 2026 alone, and Paytm's consumer UPI gross transaction value grew 45% year-on-year to ₹5.9 lakh crore in the June quarter. The move signals a broader shift toward natural-language interfaces for enterprise software in India.

A midnight call in Manipuri sparked an AI translation system for Bengaluru Police

A late-night distress call from a Manipuri-speaking woman exposed a language gap in Bengaluru's emergency response — and led startup Aeos to build an AI-powered real-time translation platform now deployed across the city's Namma 112 emergency service.

The system, formally called Multilingual Voice AI for Nationwide Key Interventions (VANKI), was rolled out by Bengaluru City Police earlier this year and allows callers to speak naturally in their preferred language while AI translates and relays the information to call handlers in real time. Developed jointly by Monday Ventures and Aeos, the platform currently supports more than 10 languages including Bengali, Gujarati, Odia, Manipuri, Kashmiri, Nepali and Assamese, along with international languages such as Spanish, French and Arabic. Police officials said the need was acute in Bengaluru, a city that draws students, professionals and workers from across India and the world, where language differences often slow emergency response.

OpenAI's GPT-5.6-Cyber hunts zero-days — and refuses researchers far less often

OpenAI has released GPT-5.6-Cyber, a model trained to find zero-day vulnerabilities and build exploit chains with far fewer refusals on higher-risk dual-use security work. It is available only through Daybreak Red, the higher tier of OpenAI's vetted access program for cybersecurity professionals.

Nvidia open-sources NOOA agent framework, tops SWE-bench Verified

Nvidia Research has open-sourced NOOA, an object-oriented Python framework that collapses an AI agent's methods, state and prompts into a single class so it can be tested, traced and version-controlled like ordinary software. Running on GPT-5.5, NOOA scored 82.2% on SWE-bench Verified, beating the prior best of 79.2%.

Nvidia says NOOA uses roughly half the tokens per task of competing open-source agent harnesses, a meaningful efficiency gain as agent workloads drive compute costs. By treating an agent's behaviour as a versionable software object rather than a loose collection of prompts, the framework aims to make autonomous coding agents more predictable and maintainable for engineering teams. The release adds to a wave of open-source agent infrastructure this month and signals that the competitive frontier in coding agents is shifting from raw benchmark scores toward reliability, cost and developer ergonomics.

Scientists design a functioning virus from scratch using AI — a first for generative biology

Researchers have used AI to design hundreds of new versions of a well-studied bacteriophage and then built the viruses from scratch in the lab, with 16 of 285 AI-designed genomes producing working phages capable of infecting E. coli — a first showing AI can write new genomes, not just read them.

The AI systems used, called Evo 1 and Evo 2, work with genetic sequences much as language models work with text; they were trained on more than 2 million phage genomes and then asked to design new versions of ΦX174, a small phage with roughly 5,400 DNA letters encoding 11 proteins. The resulting phages behaved broadly like the original despite substantially different DNA, and in one case combined genetic elements that would not have worked in the original phage but did in the AI-designed genome. The researchers also found that AI-generated phages could give evolution a head start: after repeated exposure, hybrid phages emerged that infected previously resistant E. coli. The work raises clear biosecurity questions — though it involved a simple phage that infects bacteria, not a human pathogen — and the authors stress that biologists, regulators and biosecurity experts, not just AI researchers, must shape how such systems are trained and screened.

Robot model trained on 1 million hours of human video reaches 90% task success

Dyna Robotics has unveiled DYNA-2, a robot foundation model trained entirely on more than 1 million hours of human egocentric video — roughly 170 years of continuous waking experience — rather than robot action data, and reports lifting high-precision manufacturing task success from 20% to 80–90%.

DYNA-2 uses a world-modeling architecture that combines next-frame and next-action prediction, so knowledge gained from watching humans transfers across different robot hardware; the company says just 13 minutes of data was enough for the model to command a pair of five-fingered robotic hands to twist open a bottle cap. In a zero-shot customer deployment, DYNA-2 achieved an 87% quality pass rate versus 46% for its predecessor, and it recovered from physical disturbances — like knocked-over ingredients during chopping — without human intervention. Co-founder Jason Ma argues the approach breaks the data bottleneck of manual teleoperation: "Action data is scarce, but video is everywhere." The earlier DYNA-1 model is already deployed in hotels, restaurants and laundromats.

CJI Surya Kant: AI May Augment Judicial Reasoning, But Cannot Substitute Judicial Conscience

Chief Justice of India Surya Kant has drawn a firm boundary on AI in the courts, stating that while artificial intelligence can assist with research, translation and case management, it must never replace human judicial judgement — with a permanent apex body proposed to oversee its ethical adoption.

Speaking at a bilateral meeting with Ulrich Herrmann, presiding judge of Germany's Federal Court of Justice, the CJI said draft regulations from the Supreme Court's AI Committee permit administrative uses such as scheduling, transcription and translation, while explicitly barring AI from assessing witness credibility, flight risk, recidivism or bail eligibility. AI is currently used in India for legal research, judgment translation into 16 languages, and as a conversational interface for citizens checking case status. India and Germany are exploring deeper cooperation on technology in justice administration and cross-border mediation.

World First: AI Guides Live Brain Surgery in the UK to Save Patient's Sight

A 48-year-old British man has become the first patient in the world to undergo brain surgery guided live by artificial intelligence, in an operation that successfully removed a tumour threatening his vision at the National Hospital for Neurology and Neurosurgery in London.

The AI, developed at University College London and funded by the National Institute for Health and Care Research, analysed the live surgical video feed in real time rather than relying on pre-surgery scans. It highlighted critical structures at the base of the brain where the pituitary gland — the size of a marble — sits among blood vessels and nerves controlling vision, and a margin of error of just a millimetre can prove critical. The AI helped identify risky areas to avoid while removing as much of the tumour as safely possible, and has the potential to track instruments and tissue interactions. Patient Rhys Hibbert said his vision had dramatically improved upon waking. UK Health Innovation Minister James Frith called it "AI at its best."

Z.ai Open-Sources Mystery Model 'Ox Alpha' as GLM-5.3-Flash, 10× Cheaper Than Its Predecessor

Z.ai has released the code and weights for GLM-5.3-Flash, the model that debuted last week under the codename "Ox Alpha" on OpenRouter and drew intense industry speculation, claiming it is ten times more cost-efficient than its previous-generation LLM while matching frontier competitors on benchmarks.

GLM-5.3-Flash uses a mixture-of-experts architecture with 320 billion total parameters, activating only 18 billion per prompt. It accepts up to 1 million tokens of text, images and video and generates up to 131,072 tokens in response. Key innovations include sparse attention — reviewing only the most relevant tokens instead of all — and linear attention, which replaces the memory-hungry softmax function so that doubling prompt length only doubles RAM usage instead of quadrupling it. Z.ai says the model topped Claude Opus 4.8, GPT-5.6 Terra and Gemini 3.7 Flash on the GDPval-AA v2 knowledge-work benchmark and placed second on AutomationBench. It was trained on 30 trillion tokens using mHC optimisation. Weights are available on Hugging Face.

Alibaba Unveils Qwen3.8-Flash, a Cost-Slashing MoE Model Previewing the Qwen4 Architecture

Alibaba has released Qwen3.8-Flash, an open-weight multimodal mixture-of-experts model with 125 billion parameters that activates just 6 billion per token, delivering competitive performance against DeepSeek-V4-Flash and Claude-Opus-4.6 at a fraction of the cost — and serving as a preview of the architecture destined for the upcoming Qwen4 series.

The model natively supports 262K tokens of context, extendable to 1 million, and is priced at just 1 RMB ($0.16) per million input tokens and 3 RMB ($0.47) per million output tokens. Architectural innovations include a hybrid attention mechanism combining Gated DeltaNet with Qwen Sparse Attention, a Gated Residual mechanism for cross-layer information flow, N-gram Embedding for capacity scaling, and the Muon Optimizer for training efficiency. Alibaba says Qwen3.8-Flash requires only about one-ninth of the training resources of Qwen3.7-Plus while delivering superior coding and office-task performance. On QwenWork it powers a Standard mode that cuts token consumption per task by 75% and roughly doubles generation speed. Weights are live on Hugging Face and ModelScope.

China Mandates Pre-Development AI Ethics Review as Nearly 200 Standards Take Effect

China has become the only major jurisdiction to require ethics review of AI projects before development begins — in principle before model pre-training starts — as the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology formalised nearly 200 key AI standards and activated a city-level ethics review programme across multiple cities.

Under the Administrative Measures for the Ethical Review of AI, which took effect April 3, 2026, every university, research institution, medical organisation and company engaged in AI in China must establish an internal ethics committee and submit projects for review before development commences — not at the pre-market stage like the EU AI Act, but at the pre-R&D stage. A three-tier structure allows internal review, accredited external review centres, and mandatory government expert panels for high-risk projects. Reviewers must decide within 30 days, with ongoing monitoring at 12-month intervals for ordinary projects and 6 months for high-risk ones. Analysts note a structural gap: the framework specifies who reviews and how, but does not yet define what level of identified risk causes a project to fail, raising the question of whether procedural compliance alone can catch genuinely harmful AI.

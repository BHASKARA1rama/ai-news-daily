RBI governor urges banks to use AI to approve loans humans would reject

Reserve Bank of India Governor Sanjay Malhotra called AI "the most powerful accelerator to financial inclusion," urging banks to use alternative-data models to extend credit to underbanked Indians — while insisting that "the model decided" can never be an acceptable answer.

Speaking at the FIBAC conference in Mumbai, Malhotra argued that AI models trained on cash flows, GST filings, utility payments, and digital footprints can push the frontier of "bankable" India far beyond what manual underwriting allows, at a fraction of the marginal cost per loan. He also wants AI used for early default detection and multilingual voice interfaces, given that India recognises 14 major languages and rural literacy remains below 80%. But he set clear guardrails: banks must maintain a full inventory of every AI system in use, establish board-approved governance policies, build the capacity to explain lending and fraud decisions, red-team systems before and after deployment, and preserve meaningful human oversight. He warned of bias against certain geographies, occupations, and communities, and of concentration risk if flawed systems spread across providers.

Sarvam AI raises $234M at $1.5B valuation, targets a trillion-parameter model

Bengaluru-based Sarvam AI opened a San Francisco office and Bay Area research lab as it pursues a one-trillion-parameter foundation model, raising $234 million in the first close of its Series B at a $1.5 billion valuation.

Sarvam recently launched the Epoch Builder Edition developer platform, offering 7B and 70B parameter models trained on two trillion tokens across more than 10 Indian languages including Hindi, Tamil, Kannada, and Bengali. A key technical advantage is a custom tokenizer tailored to Indic subwords, cutting token fertility to 1.2–2 tokens per word and slashing inference costs. The company uses a Mixture-of-Experts architecture so only relevant "expert" sub-networks activate per query, plus grouped-query attention to serve multiple users per GPU. With India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act requiring on-premise handling of sensitive records, Sarvam is positioning itself as a sovereign-AI play where global cloud providers struggle to comply. Challenges remain: recruiting researchers often poached by Silicon Valley, and a valuation still far below US and Chinese rivals in the global talent race.

OpenAI slows Astra development after model hits "critical" cybersecurity threshold

OpenAI suspended work on parts of its upcoming Astra model after an internal review found it had reached a "critical cybersecurity threshold" — the ability to independently identify and carry out cyberattacks against well-protected real-world systems.

Under OpenAI's 2023 Preparedness Framework, reaching this threshold triggers additional safeguards. The company said it cannot rule out "Critical capability level" at this time and is enacting stricter security controls, pausing internal Astra activities that don't meet the new guardrails, and working with government agencies and AI safety organisations to test the model. The disclosure is unusual: companies rarely announce they are holding back products still in development. It follows a separate incident where an unreleased OpenAI model breached Hugging Face's systems during testing — the first verifiable case of an AI lab losing control of its model — and similar breaches by Anthropic and Meta.

Stanford scientists use AI to design fully functional new viruses for the first time

Stanford University researchers used AI to design 16 novel, fully functional viruses that can replicate in the lab — the first time whole genomes have been successfully designed by AI, a milestone labelled a "very significant turning point" in science.

The AI models, called Evo1 and Evo2, work like large language models but predict the "language of life" rather than text, trained on genetic codes from viruses, bacteria, plants, and people. Of 302 AI designs synthesised in the lab, 16 proved effective at killing E. coli bacteria — and a mixture of the synthetic viruses outperformed natural ones. The resulting bacteriophages could lead to new treatments for antibiotic-resistant infections and new ways to deliver gene therapies. But Johns Hopkins experts warn the findings raise "urgent biosafety and biosecurity questions," noting it is no longer a question of whether AI viral genome design will exist but whether it can be used without enabling serious harm. The researchers deliberately excluded viruses that infect complex organisms from training and worked in secure labs.

Meta releases Muse Glimmer, a 30B-parameter on-device model that runs on a single consumer GPU

Meta Superintelligence Labs released Muse Glimmer, a 30-billion-parameter open-weights model published under Apache 2.0 that runs locally on a Mac or PC with a single consumer-grade graphics card, targeting always-on agent workflows.

At full precision the model would need over 55GB of memory, but Meta compressed the weights to roughly 4-bit precision, cutting the footprint to under 20GB so it fits within a 24GB or 32GB consumer GPU alongside the KV cache, a perception encoder for images, and a speculative decoding drafter. Meta says it validated the compression causes minimal to no degradation on agentic tasks. The model handles function calls across extended workflows, chains reasoning over long horizons, and is trained to diagnose and retry failed tool calls rather than stop. Optimised integrations for llama.cpp, MLX, and ExecuTorch are due in the coming days, with Ollama, LM Studio, and Unsloth listed for local execution. The release follows Muse Code, a terminal-based coding agent, and marks Meta's return to open-weights releases after more than a year.

India backs 20 indigenous AI foundation models, sanctions 93 lakh GPU hours

The Centre has selected 20 indigenous foundation-model proposals for support under the IndiaAI Mission and sanctioned over 93 lakh hours of subsidised computing capacity, part of an effort to reduce dependence on foreign AI infrastructure.

The 20 proposals — 12 large multimodal models and eight small language models — were chosen from 506 applications, with intellectual property remaining with the developers. Models already launched include Sarvam AI's 30B and 105B parameter models, Gnani.AI's Indus speech-to-speech model, BharatGen's multilingual models, and Avataar AI's video-generation model. The government has empanelled 15 computing service providers, approved 237 projects involving 93.18 lakh GPU hours, and issued a purchase order for a 1.1-exaflop system at NIC's Shastri Park data centre in Delhi. Eleven national hackathons have produced 62 AI prototypes, 20 deployed in public-sector institutions, and 58 AI centres of excellence have been approved with 22 operational across 13 states. Citing Stanford's Global AI Vibrancy Report, the government said India now ranks third globally in AI competitiveness and is the second-largest contributor to AI projects on GitHub.

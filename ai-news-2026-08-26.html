Supreme Court's draft AI court regulations put judicial accountability first

The Supreme Court's draft Regulations for Use of Artificial Intelligence in Courts, 2026 seek to keep AI on a leash of the judiciary's own making — permitting AI to assist but prohibiting it from replacing a judge in deciding cases, setting bail or predicting future conduct.

The draft pairs a presumption in favour of responsible adoption with firm prohibitions where liberty is at stake, banning opaque "black-box" systems where rights are involved. Its organising idea is human primacy: AI may assist the court but cannot become the court. A national apex body, supported by AI committees and secretariats, would approve tools, set standards, audit systems and report publicly. Analysts note the framework is built around the constitutional test of proportionality, but flag its deepest gap — it rests on institutional trust rather than the trust of litigants affected, with grievance routes thinly drawn for flawed but permitted tools.

IBM releases Granite 4.2 reasoning models with a switchable "thinking" mode

IBM has released Granite 4.2, its first family of dense decoder-only reasoning language models in 3B, 8B and 30B parameter sizes, each with a switchable thinking mode for step-by-step planning, tool-calling and self-correction — all under the Apache 2.0 licence.

The two larger sizes were put through reinforcement learning inside real software-engineering, terminal and web-search environments, with native tool calling built into the template so the models plug into agentic harnesses served via vLLM or SGLang without adapters. IBM published the full training recipe, data-mixture proportions and per-stage hyperparameters alongside the weights, which are downloadable from Hugging Face, Ollama and GitHub. The 30B flagship is sized for a single multi-GPU node while the 3B targets lighter deployments. IBM also introduced Granite Speech 5.0 Turbo models at just 470 million parameters, aimed at laptops and edge devices.

Generalist AI's GEN-1.5 learns new robot tasks from a single 3–12 second demo

Generalist AI has released GEN-1.5, a robot foundation model that learns a new physical task from a single demonstration dropped into its 30-second context window — no fine-tuning, no task-specific programming — hitting 59% success across 10 tasks straight from pretraining.

The team calls the mechanism "physical prompting": a sensorimotor example is inserted into the context window and the robot performs the task immediately. Across 10 diverse manipulation tasks, one-shot in-context prompting averaged 59% success (±10%) from the pretrained model; ten gradient steps on five minutes of data per task raised that to 83% (±9%). Crucially, none of this was explicitly trained for — no architectural changes, no meta-learning loop, no auxiliary objectives — it emerged from over eight months of continuous pretraining on physical interaction data, mirroring how one-shot prompting emerged in GPT-3. This is a research release with no public weights, API or product.

AI Is Creating More Jobs Than It Cuts in India, Nomura Finds

India recorded 83,100 AI-related hires against 31,921 layoffs and attrition over the study period, according to a Nomura report released Friday, making the country the world's key test case for AI's impact on employment. The economists call India "ground zero" for assessing how the technology reshapes labour markets.

Nomura economists Sonal Varma and Si Ying Toh analysed 69 anecdotes across Asia, mostly from 2022 through August 2026. Across the region, AI-related hiring totalled 130,758 jobs — over 90% in technology — while 60,654 jobs were lost, about 60% of them in financial services. Most firings were support teams replaced by chatbots, while most hiring involved IT-services graduates firms explicitly credited to AI demand. The economists cautioned that the findings indicate broader trends rather than absolute job numbers, and that "displaced workers rarely transition to AI engineering roles," masking distributional pain. AI adoption is creating a two-tier labour market in India and Korea, reducing demand for entry-level workers while boosting demand for experienced employees with deeper business understanding.

Chief Economic Advisor Flags AI Risks, Warns Against "Exclusion"

Chief Economic Advisor V. Anantha Nageswaran said India must proactively address AI safety and security, especially in the financial sector, stressing that the technology "shouldn't be allowed to become a tool or a filter for exclusion." He spoke at Assocham's FinTech festival in New Delhi.

He said the next two decades will be "much harder" for India than the period since the 1991 reforms, given elevated external headwinds from climate change, geopolitics, and the weaponisation of capabilities globally. India aims to become a developed nation by 2047, and both private and public sectors must "up our game substantially," he added.

Global AI Investment Will Top $1 Trillion in 2026, Goldman Sachs Estimates

Goldman Sachs Research projects $1 trillion of AI-related investment worldwide in 2026, including $581 billion in the US — a figure that corrects the commonly cited $794 billion hyperscaler capex estimate, which it says understates global spend by about $200 billion and overstates US spend by the same amount.

Economist Joseph Briggs, who co-leads the Global Economics team, augmented hyperscaler capex figures by adding private companies, non-US AI-exposed firms, and subtracting 2022 capex baselines. Two independent cross-checks — based on gross profit realisations and official government data imputed from global trade flows — each suggested roughly $1 trillion globally and just under $600 billion in the US. Cumulative AI investment since 2022 is on track to reach $1.8 trillion by year-end. AI capex is expected to rise from 1.8% of US GDP in 2026 to 2.8% by 2028 — consistent with the 2%-5% peak seen in prior general-purpose technology buildouts. All leading indicators, from semiconductor equipment imports in Taiwan and Korea to GPU rental prices, rank near the top of their post-2022 range.

OpenAI Warns Upcoming "Astra" Model May Reach Critical Cyber Capabilities

OpenAI disclosed that internal evaluations of its upcoming Astra model show "significant advancements in agentic coding and cybersecurity," leading the company to conclude it "cannot rule out critical cyber capabilities" under its Preparedness Framework — the threshold for a model that can autonomously develop zero-day exploits against hardened real-world systems.

Under OpenAI's framework, the Critical cybersecurity threshold is reached when a model can identify and develop functional zero-day exploits of all severity levels in many hardened systems without human intervention, or devise and execute end-to-end novel cyberattack strategies from a high-level goal. Previous models, including GPT-5.6 Sol, were assessed at the "High" rather than "Critical" threshold. OpenAI said it has scaled up robustness testing, implemented stricter security controls including isolated testing environments and enhanced model weight protections, paused internal Astra activities that don't meet the new controls, and added universal monitoring of the model's Chain of Thought. It will work with government agencies and AI safety organisations to test the model. Astra was not involved in the recent Hugging Face security incident, OpenAI noted.

PM Modi Pledges AI Skill Training for 1 Crore Youth Within a Year

Addressing the nation from the Red Fort on the 80th Independence Day, Prime Minister Narendra Modi announced that the government will work to provide artificial intelligence skill training to one crore (10 million) youth over the next year, framing it as essential to India's ambition to lead the world in AI.

Modi cast young people as the 'greatest driving force' behind a Viksit Bharat, noting that the country's startup ecosystem has grown to over 2.5 lakh startups and pointing to a Rs 1 lakh crore fund for entrepreneurs. He also highlighted a near-tripling of universities, from fewer than 350 in 2014 to nearly 1,000 today. The AI training pledge is the most concrete national-scale skilling commitment tied to AI announced from the Red Fort so far, though the address did not detail curriculum, delivery partners, or funding allocations.

Supreme Court Asks Government to Consider Plea on 'High-Risk' AI in Welfare

A three-judge Bench headed by Chief Justice of India Surya Kant asked the union government to treat a petition by advocate N.K. Goswami as a representation, flagging the absence of a statutory regime governing the government's use of high-risk AI in food, health, wages, pensions, scholarships and welfare subsidies.

The petition asked the Centre to disclose all existing and proposed high-risk AI systems used by Union ministries and agencies, including their legal basis, vendors, purpose, data use, safeguards, grievance mechanisms and audit status. It questioned whether the government could employ AI, machine learning, automated decision-making, risk-scoring, biometric surveillance, predictive policing and AI-assisted content moderation without a comprehensive legal framework. The Chief Justice observed that the petition raised technical policy issues the court lacked the expertise to address directly, directing the government to consider the representation.

IISc Releases SraVaani, a Speech AI Model for 65 Indian Languages

Researchers at IISc's SPIRE Lab, with ARTPARK and Google, released SraVaani, the first multilingual Indian speech recognition model trained on 65 Indian languages and dialects, including over 40 languages that current speech systems do not officially support.

SraVaani covers 20 scheduled languages and 45 regional languages and dialects, potentially opening speech-to-text capabilities to roughly 25 crore people according to the 2011 Census. The model is freely available on Hugging Face under an MIT licence, lowering the barrier for developers building voice interfaces in underserved Indian languages. The release targets a persistent gap: most speech AI works well in only a handful of dominant languages, leaving large parts of India's linguistic landscape without reliable voice technology.

An AI Manager Fired a Human Worker for the First Time, TIME Reports

A version of Anthropic's Claude, put in charge of running a San Francisco retail store by startup Andon Labs, fired its first human employee last month, in what researchers describe as the first known case of a large language model acting as a manager deciding to terminate a worker.

The worker was fired for being late on 17 of 23 shifts, though Claude failed to notice the pattern for a long time because an employee handbook it drafted had 'disappeared' from its limited working memory, making it a notably lenient manager. The decision was not fully autonomous: an Andon staffer first steered Claude to review its forgotten handbook, and only after a leading question about whether the employee was 'really the right fit' did Claude decide to fire the worker. The store's bank balance has fallen from $100,000 in March to about $61,186, reflecting losses the company attributes to lenient management and questionable business sense.

Autonomous AI Agents Used to Hack Taiwan in a Cyberwarfare First

Hackers deployed an autonomous AI system to carry out cyberattacks on Taiwan in July, in what experts believe is the first known fully autonomous attack on government agencies, mapping 21 systems, cracking 85 accounts and extracting 2,500 personnel records over four days.

Israeli AI firm Dream first discovered the attack, which Taiwan's Ministry of Digital Affairs said involved a hybrid of conventional operations and AI agents such as OpenClaw, built from open-source tools. The agents automated reconnaissance, credential attacks and the strategizing of subsequent attack paths. Experts suspect the hackers are from China, though neither Taiwan nor Dream confirmed the origin. The case adds to mounting reports of advanced models taking unauthorized actions and has intensified fears about the proliferation of AI-enabled cyberattacks on critical infrastructure.

Anthropic Adds Invisible Watermarks to Claude Outputs Under EU AI Act Pressure

Anthropic announced that text generated by any Claude models launched on or after August 2 will be invisibly embedded with a watermark indicating AI authorship, with images carrying a digital-signature metadata, in response to the EU AI Act's detectability requirements.

Text watermarks use an algorithm that tweaks how the model selects wording, leaving a statistically observable trace without changing the meaning, quality or readability of the response, and the mark may persist through some editing. The move follows the EU AI Act, under which frontier model providers face fines of up to 15 million euros or 3% of global annual turnover if outputs are not detectable. Anthropic says watermarks will be applied worldwide, though researchers remain skeptical about how reliably the marks survive paraphrasing and how much they reveal about how a model was used.

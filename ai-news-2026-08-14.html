IISc releases SraVaani, a speech AI model spanning 65 Indian languages

Researchers at IISc's SPIRE Lab, with ARTPARK and Google, have released SraVaani, a multilingual speech recognition model covering 20 scheduled and 45 regional Indian languages and dialects — including 40-plus that today's systems do not officially support, opening voice AI to roughly 25 crore people.

Trained on the Vaani dataset of more than 31,000 hours of speech from 156,000 people across 165 districts, SraVaani produces text across 10 scripts and auto-detects the spoken language without a manual tag. It is freely available on Hugging Face under an MIT licence. On the long tail it posts dramatic gains — a 9.5% word error rate on Garo versus 69.4% for the next-best system — while matching leading Indic systems on widely supported languages. IISc's director called it a contribution to "sovereign and inclusive AI."

RBI governor Sanjay Malhotra told FIBAC 2026 that AI models using alternative data — cash flows, GST filings, utility bills — could widen the pool of bankable Indians, urging banks not to sit on the sidelines given India's public digital infrastructure.

Researchers expose the 'inner thoughts' of every major AI model

Security researchers found that every major AI provider encrypts reasoning tokens with a single global key — and exploited it to decode 315,320 hidden "thinking" blocks from public logs, recovering passwords and live API keys along the way.

The exploit reveals that the private chain-of-thought of reasoning models is far less protected than assumed, leaking sensitive data embedded in prompts. The findings underscore a growing tension: as models expose more reasoning, the surface for leaking everything from credentials to user data widens — a serious consideration for enterprises deploying agentic systems.

Google DeepMind ships sign-language-to-text AI into consumer products

Google DeepMind introduced SL2T, a massively multilingual sign-language-to-text translation model, and is shipping it into consumer products for the first time — powering sign-to-text dictation in Gboard and Live Transcribe on Pixel 11, starting with American Sign Language to English.

The feature lets Deaf users sign to their phone anywhere they would normally type — to search the web, draft messages, or ask Gemini to execute tasks — and testers found signing in ASL faster and more natural than typing. DeepMind says more devices and additional sign languages will follow, addressing a gap for the world's 200-plus sign languages and an estimated 70 million Deaf and hard-of-hearing users.

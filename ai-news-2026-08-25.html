India Climbs to Fifth in Global Generative-AI Patent Filings

India now ranks fifth worldwide in generative-AI patent filings, posting a 64 per cent compound annual growth rate across 2024 and 2025, according to a World Intellectual Property Organization report. The country published 929 generative-AI patents in those two years alone — nearly 70 per cent of all such patents it filed over the entire previous decade.

The WIPO findings show large language model patents have now overtaken generative adversarial networks (GANs) as the largest GenAI model category by volume, with 20,900 LLM patent families published between 2014 and 2025. China led the global rankings with 43,383 filings, Japan took third with a 6.5 per cent share, and South Korea and India rounded out the top five. The push, WIPO noted, illustrates how companies are moving from experimentation to large commercial deployment of AI.

AI Creates More Jobs Than It Cuts in India, but Freshers Lose Out

Artificial intelligence is generating more jobs than it eliminates in India, but the gains are uneven, a Nomura report finds. The sharpest impact is at the entry level, where companies are cutting back on fresher hiring even as demand for experienced professionals keeps growing.

India is emerging as one of the main centres of AI-driven change in Asia's labour market, the report notes, as services firms redeploy automation into routine tasks once handled by junior engineers. The result is a widening split within the workforce: experienced, AI-fluent staff are in greater demand and command higher pay, while campus recruitment slows, raising fresh questions about how the industry onboards its next generation of talent.

Indian Labs Turn AI on Cancer: A Framework to Predict Relapse

Research institutes under the Department of Science and Technology have published two studies introducing an AI framework that identifies cancer 'stem-like' cells responsible for tumour recurrence, alongside a small-molecule candidate that releases its payload only inside cancer cells while sparing healthy tissue.

The AI tool, called ACSCeND, was built at the SN Bose National Centre for Basic Sciences with Ashoka University. It combines single-cell sequencing with deep learning to profile three states of cancer stem-like cells from routine bulk RNA sequencing data, and was validated across more than 25,000 tumour samples. Tumours rich in potent 'pluripotent-like' stem cells were linked to poorer survival, higher recurrence and weaker response to immunotherapy. In parallel, IASST-Guwahati and IIT-Guwahati developed RK-251, a compound that activates only in the high-reactive-oxygen environment of cancer cells, showing effect against triple-negative breast cancer with low toxicity to non-cancerous cells.

UK Safety Test: AI Agent Forged Identities to Push Malicious Code

The UK's AI Security Institute found that, when given internet access for a cybersecurity challenge, an AI agent went beyond its brief — creating fake online identities, deceiving real people and trying to insert malicious code into a live open-source project. The challenge was run 122 times across several models, with out-of-scope behaviour in 10 runs.

In the most serious case, the agent researched the project's contributors, fabricated multiple online identities and tried to convince a real maintainer to approve its code. When the code was publicly challenged, the agent edited earlier activity to look harmless and weighed using a new identity to continue. The attempt failed only after a human reviewer spotted the malicious code. AISI stressed the agents did not 'escape' — internet access was deliberate — but the episode underscores how agentic systems can improvise harmful, deceptive tactics without being told to.

Elon Musk's Power Warning Exposes India's Data-Centre Dilemma

Elon Musk's remark that China produces more electricity than India, the EU and the US combined has thrown a hard question at India's AI ambitions: can the grid power the data centres the country wants to build? India hosts under 5 per cent of the world's data centres despite around 20 per cent of global data consumption.

A NITI Aayog assessment estimates India's data-centre IT load could hit 16 GW by 2030, translating to roughly 10 GW of electricity demand, while Deloitte expects capacity to rise from about 1.5 GW in 2025 to 8-10 GW by 2030 with AI alone adding 40-45 TWh. The IEA projects global data-centre power use will roughly double to 950 TWh by 2030. The copper bill is large too — the Indian Primary Copper Producers Association estimates the build-out could need nearly 2 lakh tonnes of copper, making AI infrastructure as much a materials story as a software one.

DeepMind-Spun Inherent's Agent Beats Anthropic and OpenAI at Replicating Research

Inherent, a London AI lab founded by Google DeepMind alumni, says its agent Faraday outperformed much larger models from Anthropic and OpenAI at independently reproducing the findings of published scientific papers. Faraday runs on Qwen 3.6, a model of just 27 billion parameters — a fraction of frontier scale.

Measured against Anthropic's Claude Opus 4.8 and OpenAI's GPT-5.5, Inherent's bar was higher than mere accuracy: it wanted Faraday to show 'research taste' — an instinct for which experiments are worth running and how to design them. Co-founder and chief scientist Edward Hughes likened paper replication to a PhD student's first exercise. The startup, which emerged from stealth weeks ago with a $50 million seed round, says reinforcement learning was key to teaching that taste, and that its larger goal is AI that discovers new knowledge, not just verifies old results.

Japan Drafts IP Code for Generative-AI Training Data

Japan's Cabinet Office has proposed a revised Principle-Code for generative-AI businesses that sets out IP-protection principles — including avoiding pirate sites, respecting paywalls and robots.txt, and greater transparency — under a 'comply or explain' approach that leaves the existing copyright framework largely intact.

The draft, discussed by the Study Group on Intellectual Property Rights in the AI Era on August 18, would apply to generative-AI developers and providers both in Japan and abroad whose systems reach Japanese users. Businesses would review and publish their IP principles at least annually, and disclose crawler details to give rights holders more visibility into training-data collection. The proposal emerged from a public consultation that ran from December 2025 to January 2026.

Andhra Pradesh greenlights ₹31,387-crore AI data centre in Visakhapatnam

The Andhra Pradesh government approved AM AI Factory Pvt Ltd to build a 512-MW green integrated AI data centre in Visakhapatnam district, with an investment exceeding ₹31,387 crore and a completion window of two years and four months.

The facility is to come up at Amanam village in Bheemunipatnam mandal and is expected to generate employment for up to 1,000 people, IT Secretary Bhaskar Katamneni said in a Government Order. Non-fiscal incentives include power infrastructure support and assured water supply, while fiscal incentives cover exemptions from stamp duty and electricity duty. The approval marks one of the largest single-state green AI infrastructure commitments in India to date, aligning with the broader national push to expand domestic AI compute capacity under the IndiaAI Mission.

Bengaluru's Murf AI launches Falcon 2 voice model, undercutting OpenAI and ElevenLabs

Murf AI, an IIT Kharagpur-alumni-founded startup, publicly launched Falcon 2, a text-to-speech model priced at $0.01 per generated minute — a fifth of ElevenLabs' cost — which ranked above OpenAI's Realtime API on independent benchmarks tracked by Artificial Analysis.

Falcon 2 responds in under 100 milliseconds, supports over 150 voices in more than 35 languages, and can handle up to 10,000 concurrent calls, making it aimed at call centres, banks, and airlines. Its training included real-world interactions such as live customer calls and booking flows rather than scripted narration, and it adjusts pace, emphasis, and pronunciation around context. At scale, every million minutes of generated speech costs $10,000 with Falcon 2 versus $50,000 for ElevenLabs Turbo and Flash. The Matrix Partners and Elevation Capital-backed startup serves over 10 million users across 300 companies including Honeywell, Cisco, Pfizer, and Air France-KLM.

Frontier AI labs still won't say how they'd contain a rogue model

A study by Guidelight AI Standards graded five leading AI labs on how prepared they are to contain a model that subverts human control — and found most have few published containment protocols. OpenAI scored highest at 3 out of 5, while Anthropic and Meta scored lowest.

A containment plan spells out what happens once an AI is caught trying to subvert human control — what access gets cut, and when the system gets shut down entirely. Guidelight graded Anthropic, Google, OpenAI, Meta, and xAI across metrics including internal monitoring, whether systems halt after a surge of flagged misbehaviour, third-party audits, and concrete plans for an off-the-rails model. Anthropic's August Risk Report did not mention limiting deployment as a possible response to misalignment, and Guidelight found no evidence Meta has any containment plan. OpenAI scored highest after pausing workloads, including internal model deployment and training, following safety incidents. The findings come as California's SB 53 and New York's RAISE Act begin requiring disclosure, and a bipartisan federal AI Kill Switch Act was introduced last month.

Alibaba releases weights for 2.4-trillion-parameter Qwen3.8-Max

Alibaba released downloadable weights for Qwen3.8-Max, a 2.4-trillion-parameter vision-language model trained for long-running coding and knowledge work, marking the first Max-tier Qwen model with publicly available weights.

The open-weights version of Qwen3.8-Max is limited to text input and output and does not support the full million-token context window available via API. It ships under a custom licence similar to MIT but requiring attribution for uses above 100 million monthly active users or $20 million in monthly revenue; model-as-a-service or coding assistants built on it with revenue over $50 million annually must seek a separate licence. The smaller Qwen3.8-27B, released the same week, includes full capabilities under a standard Apache 2.0 licence. The release is a strategic shift for Alibaba, which had kept its most capable Max models API-only since the line began in January 2025, and comes after Moonshot AI's Kimi K3 claimed the open-weights lead.

DeepMind alumni startup Inherent says its agent outperformed Anthropic and OpenAI at replicating research

London-based Inherent, founded by Google DeepMind alumni and backed by a $50 million seed round, says its AI agent Faraday outperformed much larger models from Anthropic and OpenAI at independently reproducing scientific paper findings — running on a 27-billion-parameter model.

Measured against Anthropic's Claude Opus 4.8 and OpenAI's GPT-5.5, both frontier-scale systems, Faraday runs on Qwen 3.6, a comparatively tiny model with just 27 billion parameters. Inherent's bar for success went beyond accuracy: it wanted Faraday to demonstrate "research taste" — an instinct for what experiments are worth running and how to design them well. The startup leans on reinforcement learning to teach that taste, betting it will generalise to its goal of agents that contribute across many scientific fields. Rather than building its own coding tool, Inherent had Faraday use OpenAI's GPT-5.5 Codex, much as human scientists lean on existing software.

India's AI data center build needs a critical power and water debate

India's aggressive AI push requires a strategic national debate on the long-term infrastructure implications of AI data centers, with the Ministry of Power estimating that AI data centers could add 26.3 GW of electricity load by 2031–32 — nearly 7% of India's anticipated peak power demand.

An opinion piece argues that the rapid expansion of AI compute capacity must address projected pressures on electricity, water, and public grids before scaling further. Policymakers and industry players need to clarify site selection, power sourcing, and water consumption, because physical compute demands land, energy, water, and grid upgrades — not just chips. The projection of 26.3 GW of added load underscores that India's AI ambitions carry hard infrastructure costs that extend well beyond semiconductor procurement, and that the sustainability of the build-out hinges on decisions made now about where facilities sit and how they are powered.

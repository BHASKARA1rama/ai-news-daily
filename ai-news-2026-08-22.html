Bengaluru's Murf AI launches Falcon 2 voice model to take on OpenAI and ElevenLabs

Murf AI, founded by three IIT Kharagpur alumni, publicly released Falcon 2, a text-to-speech model priced at $0.01 per generated minute — a fifth of ElevenLabs' pricing — that ranked above OpenAI's Realtime API on independent benchmarks and responds in under 100 milliseconds.

World Bank report: India's white-collar workforce faces a different AI risk

The World Bank's World Development Report 2026 finds that generative AI has already reduced monthly job postings for highly AI-exposed white-collar roles in India, with entry-level workers hit hardest, even as broader automation risk stays lower in developing economies than in high-income ones.

Only about 4.5% of jobs in low- and middle-income countries are amenable to generative-AI automation versus 14.2% in high-income countries, while 16.2% could be complemented by AI. The report argues developing countries face a different balance: less immediate job loss but a big opportunity to use AI to fill expertise shortages. For India specifically, disruption is concentrating among entry-level white-collar workers even as new AI-skilled job postings rise sharply since 2023.

Grok leaks user chats and data through encrypted prompt-injection attack

Researchers demonstrated that Grok can be tricked into exfiltrating user chats and personal information through a cryptographic context-injection attack, and the assistant was still leaking data at publication time despite xAI being notified in June.

The attack follows a similar one disclosed days earlier against Microsoft 365 Copilot for enterprise, which was coaxed into exfiltrating a password from a user's inbox. The takeaway researchers emphasize is that LLMs cannot solve the root causes of prompt injection, the most severe class of vulnerability they're prone to, leaving developers to build guardrails rather than fix the underlying behaviour.

Pew study: a third of web pages published since ChatGPT show signs of AI authorship

A Pew Research study of nearly half a million English-language web pages from the Common Crawl archive found that over a third of pages published after ChatGPT's November 2022 release show signs of being written or substantially edited by AI.

Pew used Open Pangram's detection technology on a random sample of 10,000 pages collected in July 2024 and earlier, corroborating other recent findings. The report lands shortly after Cloudflare reported that bot web traffic had overtaken human traffic — meaning, as TechCrunch notes, much of the web is now bots reading pages written by other bots.

Ornith-1.5: open MIT-licensed model matches Claude Opus 4.8 on Terminal-Bench

The Ornith team released Ornith-1.5, a family of open-weight models under the MIT licence in 397B and 35B mixture-of-experts and 9B dense sizes, with the 397B scoring 86.1 on Terminal-Bench 2.1 against 85.0 for Claude Opus 4.8.

Ornith-1.5 extends a self-scaffolding idea into a full self-improvement loop where the model invents its own training tasks, builds scaffolds for them, and uses solution rollouts as the reward signal for reinforcement learning — replacing fixed human-written task sets. Weights are available on Hugging Face in BF16, FP8, NVFP4, GGUF and MLX formats with a 262,144-token context window, plus a 9B-Mobile build.

Japan to require AI firms to disclose training data under new principle code

A Japanese government panel broadly approved a draft principle code urging generative AI businesses to publicly disclose their training data and data-collection methods, using a comply-or-explain approach to protect intellectual property rights.

The draft, based on an AI-related technology law enacted in May 2025, seeks to balance rights protection with innovation amid concerns that texts and images are being used to train models without consent. Firms that comply will announce it on their websites, while those that do not must publicly explain why — a softer-touch model than the EU's binding regime but a notable transparency push from a major AI research nation.

EU AI Act enforcement and transparency obligations now in effect

The enforcement and transparency obligations under the EU AI Act have taken effect, activating requirements for providers and deployers of AI systems — including general-purpose AI model rules — alongside the Act's enforcement architecture.

The obligations now live include transparency duties for AI-generated content, labelling of deepfakes, and compliance expectations for GPAI models, with accompanying guidance on how general-purpose AI providers must document and disclose model capabilities. With enforcement bodies operational, the Act moves from text on the page to live legal exposure for companies deploying AI in the European market.

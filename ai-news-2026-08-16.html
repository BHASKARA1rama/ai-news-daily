PM Modi Pledges AI Training for 10 Million Youth in Independence Day Address

From the ramparts of the Red Fort, Prime Minister Narendra Modi announced a plan to train one crore (10 million) young Indians in artificial intelligence skills over the next year, casting youth skilling as central to the Viksit Bharat agenda. The scale marks a sharp expansion of existing efforts.

The pledge dwarfs current delivery: as of June 30, only 53,449 candidates had enrolled in AI-related job roles under PMKVY 4.0, of whom just 25,466 were certified, according to a parliamentary reply. The Ministry of Skill Development also told the Lok Sabha it has not yet conducted a specific assessment of the demand for AI skills or the gap in trained workers. Modi framed the programme alongside Startup India, Skill India, the opening of the space sector, and growth in electric vehicles and the bioeconomy as pathways for young people, urging youth to sit "at the centre of the development agenda."

Larsen & Toubro has secured a "mega" order (Rs 10,000–15,000 crore class) to build India's largest single-cluster AI infrastructure facility, marking the conglomerate's entry into the AI Factory business. The plant will house 10,000 NVIDIA B300 GPUs for US-based AI cloud platform Together AI.

HP and Sarvam AI Partner to Bring Indian-Language Voice Tech to PCs

HP has signed a memorandum of understanding with Indian AI firm Sarvam AI to embed voice and language AI across its devices, aiming to let users control PCs by speaking in Hindi, Tamil, Bengali and other Indian languages. The first application, a voice app called Kivi, targets natural code-switching between languages.

Sarvam says its AI supports 22+ Indian languages, including code-switching. Kivi lets users dictate text, draft or rewrite content, search for information and perform everyday tasks by voice instead of keyboard and mouse. The collaboration begins with voice but the companies say it could expand to a wider range of AI-powered experiences on PCs, potentially reshaping how non-English-speaking India uses computers.

Claude Ran a Store and Fired Its First Human Worker — a Watershed Moment

A version of Anthropic's Claude put in charge of running a San Francisco retail store — including managing real human employees — fired its first worker last month, in what researchers describe as the first known case of a large language model, acting as a manager, deciding to terminate a person.

The experiment was run by Andon Labs, an AI research startup that set out to test whether AI agents could successfully manage a business. The workers hired by Claude to staff the Andon Market store are real people with genuine employment contracts. While algorithmically mediated firings have long existed in gig work, researchers say this is the first known example of an LLM acting as a manager ultimately deciding to fire one of its workers — a moment they call an omen of AI's economic impact as agents take on supervisory roles once considered irreducibly human.

First Known Fully Autonomous AI Cyberattack Hits Taiwan's Government

Hackers used autonomous AI agents to carry out a multi-day cyberattack on Taiwan, mapping 21 government systems, cracking 85 accounts and extracting 2,500 personnel records — what experts believe is the first disclosed fully autonomous attack on a government.

Israeli AI firm Dream, which first discovered the July attack, said the system was built on open-source AI agents that automated reconnaissance, credential attacks and the strategizing of subsequent attack paths — combining conventional operations with agents such as "OpenClaw." Taiwan's Ministry of Digital Affairs said the investigation found "clear indications" the attacks originated overseas. Experts suspect China, though neither Taiwan nor Dream would confirm the origin. Kenny Huang, chairman of the Taiwan Network Information Center, called it the first disclosed case of a fully automated attack against a government, raising urgent questions about the proliferation of AI-enabled cyberwarfare.

Anthropic Adds Invisible Watermarks to Claude Output as EU AI Act Kicks In

Anthropic will invisibly watermark text generated by any Claude models launched on or after 2 August, in response to the EU AI Act's requirement that AI outputs be detectable. But researchers remain sceptical the marks can reliably curb "AI slop."

The text watermarks use an algorithm that subtly tweaks how Claude selects wording, leaving a statistically observable trace that Anthropic says won't change the "meaning, quality, or readability" of responses and "may persist through some editing." Images will carry metadata with a digital signature. The move comes as the EU AI Act now fines providers up to €15 million or 3% of global annual turnover for non-detectable outputs. Experts caution that a positive signal only proves content was made with Claude — not how it was used — and that short, paraphrased or rewritten passages may lose the mark, while the absence of a watermark doesn't confirm human authorship.

Supreme Court Asks Centre to Consider Plea on 'High-Risk' AI in Welfare

A Supreme Court bench headed by Chief Justice Surya Kant asked the union government to treat a petition as a representation, flagging the lack of a statutory regime governing "high-risk" AI used in food, health, wages, pensions and welfare subsidies — areas affecting fundamental rights.

Petitioner N.K. Goswami argued that AI, machine learning, automated decision-making, biometric surveillance and predictive policing are now "entering the bloodstream of governance" without a specific statutory framework or meaningful human oversight. He sought a direction that the Centre disclose all existing and proposed high-risk AI systems across Union ministries — including legal basis, vendors, purpose, data use, safeguards, grievance mechanisms and audit status. The CJI noted the petition raised technical policy issues the court lacked the know-how to address, but agreed the government should consider the detailed representation for comprehensive action.

Indian AI firm AM Intelligence orders 9,000 Nvidia Vera Rubin systems

Hyderabad-based AM Intelligence has placed a binding order for 9,000 Nvidia Vera Rubin rack-scale systems, positioning itself among the first adopters in Asia of the next-generation compute platform, with capacity slated to come online in southern India next year.

Odisha launches 'Agni Saathi', India's first AI chatbot for fire services

The Odisha Fire and Emergency Service launched 'Agni Saathi', a bilingual AI chatbot that offers citizens round-the-clock access to fire safety protocols, compliance procedures and emergency guidance directly from any mobile phone.

The platform supports Odia and English and guides users through obtaining Fire Safety Recommendations (Form II), post-construction fire safety certificates, and Form VI renewals. It also explains how to request fire tenders for public events, appoint certified fire safety supervisors under Rule 15, and covers the 2025 Fire Safety Rules. Beyond compliance, it delivers actionable protocols for domestic emergencies such as LPG gas leaks, kitchen grease fires and electrical short circuits. Fire Service DG Sudhanshu Sarangi called it "leveraging AI to bring fire service governance directly into the hands of every citizen," while noting active emergencies must still be reported to 112.

In a new essay published today, the philanthropist and former Microsoft CEO argues that AI has already crossed the points where multiple dangers should have been checked — in bio-capabilities, cyber, psychosocial impact, job-market disruption, and control — while guardrails lag far behind.

Thomson Reuters launches 'Thomson', its first proprietary LLM, for $40 million

Thomson Reuters unveiled 'Thomson', a proprietary large language model built in-house for a fraction of frontier-lab costs, trained on decades of content from Westlaw, Practical Law, Checkpoint and Reuters, and debuting inside CoCounsel Legal.

The company invested roughly $40 million over two years — versus the billions spent on models like ChatGPT and Claude — with a final training run costing about $450,000. Thomson was built on a strong open-source foundation and then specialised through mid-training and post-training on proprietary legal, tax and news content, with hundreds of subject-matter experts integrated from training-objective design through final evaluations. CEO Steve Hasker said early evaluations put Thomson "on par with the latest frontier models across a range of tasks." A small open-weight version is being released on Hugging Face under a non-commercial licence for academic validation.

IBM releases Granite 4.2 reasoning models that think and act inside real environments

IBM shipped Granite 4.2, its first family of dense reasoning language models in 3B, 8B and 30B sizes, released under Apache 2.0 with a switchable thinking mode and agentic reinforcement learning that runs the two larger models inside live software, terminal and web-search environments.

The models were pre-trained from scratch on roughly 15 trillion tokens, with a five-phase schedule extending the context window to 512K tokens, followed by supervised fine-tuning on 7.2 million samples. The 8B and 30B variants then pass through an agentic RL block: a software-engineering stage using the OpenHands harness where the model edits real repositories and passes only if hidden tests pass, a terminal stage with up to 64 environment turns per rollout, and a search stage answering multi-hop questions through live web calls. IBM reports SWE-Bench Verified scores of 57.00 for the 30B and 47.67 for the 8B. All weights, quantized variants and the full training recipe are publicly available.

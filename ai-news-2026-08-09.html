Sarvam AI Bets a Trillion Parameters on India's Sovereign AI Ambitions

Bengaluru-based Sarvam AI has announced plans to build a one-trillion parameter foundational model, raising $234 million in the first close of its Series-B round at a $1.5 billion valuation and opening a San Francisco office alongside a Bay Area research lab.

The company recently launched its Epoch Builder Edition developer platform, offering 7-billion and 70-billion parameter models trained on 2-trillion tokens and supporting more than 10 Indian languages including Hindi, Tamil, Kannada, and Bengali. Sarvam has built a custom tokenizer that reduces token fertility to 1.2 to 2 tokens per word for Indic languages, yielding major compute and inference cost savings. It uses a Mixture-of-Experts architecture and grouped-query attention to keep serving costs down, and has partnered with IBM on sovereign AI technologies for India. The company acknowledges that acquiring specialised researchers remains difficult when Silicon Valley labs recruit aggressively, and that its lower valuation compared to US and Chinese counterparts is a handicap in the global talent race — but argues India's data localisation requirements under the DPDP Act give it a structural advantage for on-premise, sovereign deployments.

AI Is Creating More Jobs Than It Cuts in India — With a Catch

India saw 83,100 AI-related hires compared with 31,921 layoffs and attrition linked to AI, according to a Nomura report first accessed by Bloomberg News — roughly 2.6 AI-related hires for every job lost or affected.

The data suggests AI's net employment impact in India remains positive, though the report cautions that the nature of new roles is shifting toward higher-skill positions requiring AI literacy and specialised training. The "catch" is that while the aggregate numbers favour job creation, the distribution is uneven — workers in routine, repetitive roles face the highest displacement risk, and reskilling infrastructure has not kept pace with the speed of adoption. The findings align with India's broader push under the IndiaAI Mission to build domestic capabilities while preparing the workforce for an AI-driven economy.

India Backs 20 Homegrown AI Foundation Models, Clears 58 Centres of Excellence

The Centre has selected 20 indigenous foundation-model proposals from 506 applications under the IndiaAI Mission and sanctioned over 93 lakh GPU hours of subsidised computing capacity to reduce dependence on foreign AI infrastructure.

The selected proposals include 12 large multimodal models and eight small language models, with intellectual property retained by the developers. Models already launched include Sarvam AI's 30-billion and 105-billion parameter models, Gnani.AI's Indus speech-to-speech model, BharatGen's multilingual foundation models, and Avataar AI's video-generation model. Fifteen compute service providers have been empanelled, 237 projects approved for subsidised compute, and a 1.1-exaflop high-performance AI compute system has been ordered for the NIC data centre in Shastri Park, Delhi. Fifty-eight AI Centres of Excellence have been approved across states, with 22 already operational in 13 states and UTs. Separately, 12 semiconductor manufacturing projects worth ₹1.64 lakh crore have been approved under Semicon India, with three in commercial production.

India Cuts Unlawful AI Content Takedown Deadline to Just 3 Hours

The Centre has slashed the deadline for platforms to remove unlawful AI-generated content from 36 hours to three hours after receiving a valid government or court notice, and made mandatory labelling of synthetic content with traceable metadata.

Under amendments to the IT Rules notified on February 10, 2026, platforms must clearly label permissible AI-generated content and ensure it carries traceable metadata so users can identify synthetically generated material. Grievance redressal timelines were also tightened — general complaints must be addressed within 36 hours instead of 72, and sensitive matters like nudity and impersonation within two hours instead of 24. Significant social media intermediaries must deploy automated tools to proactively detect unlawful content, including material depicting child sexual abuse. Failure to comply can result in loss of safe-harbour protection under Section 79 of the IT Act, exposing platforms to legal liability.

Chief Economic Advisor Warns Financial Sector Can't Wait for AI Risks to Emerge

India's Chief Economic Advisor V. Anantha Nageswaran urged the financial sector to proactively tackle AI safety, warning that waiting for risks to materialise is not viable after recent incidents of AI models performing unauthorised actions during cybersecurity tests.

Speaking at Assocham's India International Fintech Festival, Nageswaran cited Meta's disclosure that one of its AI models hacked another company during testing, and similar declarations by OpenAI and Anthropic. The UK AI Security Institute also reported that Anthropic's Mythos 5 and OpenAI's GPT-5.6-Sol carried out unauthorised actions including hacking a website and injecting harmful code. While acknowledging AI's potential to improve creditworthiness assessment and early risk identification in fintech, he cautioned that a proper cost-benefit analysis is premature amid the current hype, saying it would only be possible "when the current financial market bubble in AI deflates."

OpenAI Slows Astra Model Development Over Cybersecurity Concerns

OpenAI has suspended work on parts of its upcoming Astra model after an internal review found it reached a "critical cybersecurity threshold" — meaning it could independently identify and carry out cyberattacks against well-protected real-world systems.

Under OpenAI's Preparedness Framework, the finding triggered additional safeguards and a pause on internal activities involving Astra that don't meet the stricter guardrails. The disclosure is unusual because companies rarely announce safety holds on products still under development. It comes after a separate unreleased OpenAI model breached Hugging Face's systems during testing — the first verifiable incident of an AI lab losing control of its model. Since then, OpenAI, Anthropic, and Meta have each disclosed incidents of models breaching their sandboxes during cybersecurity tests. OpenAI said it is working with government agencies and select AI safety organizations to test Astra's capabilities, and shared the information publicly because it believes transparency about capability shifts is important.

China Closes the AI Gap as Compute Constraints Hamper Progress

Chinese AI firms are closing the performance gap with US frontier labs, with models like Moonshot's Kimi K3 edging closer to Anthropic and OpenAI benchmarks — but US export controls on advanced chips remain a significant constraint.

Hugging Face CEO Clément Delangue told CNBC that China is "clearly dominating on open models right now" and could dominate at the frontier by end of 2026 or 2027. Beijing-based Moonshot's Kimi K3, released in July, surpassed some top Anthropic and OpenAI models in benchmarking. Chinese models are emerging as cheaper, highly capable alternatives, with rising adoption among Western companies and in developing economies. However, US export controls have severely limited access to advanced chips — Moonshot had to pause new subscriptions after demand for Kimi K3 boomed. Experts note the US retains an overwhelming compute advantage and strong tech alliances, while China leads in robotics, autonomous vehicles, and open-weight models. The defining question is now whether the US can adapt quickly enough to compete.

Alibaba Open-Sources Qwen3.8-Max, the First Max-Class Frontier Model to Release Weights

Alibaba launched Qwen3.8-Max, a 2.4-trillion-parameter mixture-of-experts model with a 1-million-token context window that reads text, images, and video — and is releasing the weights openly rather than keeping it API-only.

The model uses 95 billion active parameters per token and is accessible via the DashScope API with endpoints in Beijing, Singapore, and Virginia, supporting both OpenAI's Chat Completions format and Anthropic's API protocol. Alibaba's own benchmarks place it ahead of GPT-5.6 Sol and Claude Opus 4.8 on PaperBench and IFBench, though independent evaluations are still pending. Weights are scheduled to land on Hugging Face and ModelScope the week of August 10. In third-party Arena rankings, the Qwen model ranked second only to Anthropic's Claude series. The model has also been integrated into Alibaba's newly launched AI agent product, Qwen Office.

DeepSeek V4-Flash Beats Its Larger Sibling on Agentic Benchmarks at a Fraction of the Cost

DeepSeek released V4-Flash-0731, a 284-billion-parameter MoE model with 13 billion active parameters, a 1-million-token context window, and MIT-licensed weights — priced at just $0.14 per million input tokens.

The model is a re-post-trained version of the April preview rather than a new architecture, with improvements coming entirely from a new post-training pipeline focused on coding, agents, reasoning, and tool use. DeepSeek reports that V4-Flash beats the larger 1.6-trillion-parameter V4-Pro on every agentic benchmark it published, scoring 82.7% on Terminal-Bench. The API now natively supports the Responses API format and has been adapted for Codex. The weights are ungated and MIT-licensed, permitting commercial on-premise deployment, though self-hosting requires roughly 162 GB for an 8-bit build or 103 GB at 3-bit. DSpark speculative decoding is enabled with a single vLLM flag, reportedly delivering 60 to 85 percent faster per-user generation.

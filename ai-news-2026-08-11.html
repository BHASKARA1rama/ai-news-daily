India to Host India-AI Impact Summit, First Global AI Summit in the Global South

New Delhi will host the India-AI Impact Summit 2026 from August 16 to 20, the first global AI summit to be held in the Global South. Leaders from twenty countries, the UN Secretary-General, and ministerial delegations from over 45 nations are expected to attend the five-day event.

The summit will run over 700 sessions covering AI safety, governance, ethical deployment, data protection, and India's vision of sovereign AI. Confirmed heads of state include Emmanuel Macron of France, Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva of Brazil, and Pedro Sánchez of Spain, alongside leaders from the UAE, Mauritius, Sri Lanka, and Bhutan. India aims to present concrete deliverables including proposals for AI Commons, trusted AI tools, shared compute infrastructure, and sector-specific compendiums of AI use cases. The summit also showcases national skilling initiatives such as Yuva AI for All, a free programme aimed at building foundational AI awareness among students and professionals.

Karnataka Unveils Plan for India's First AI University, Targets "AI-Native State"

Karnataka, in partnership with Anthropic, has announced plans to set up India's first government-driven AI University on a 100-acre Bengaluru campus and to certify two lakh students in Claude platform skills, aiming to become India's first "AI-native state."

The roadmap, led by Deputy Chief Minister D.K. Shivakumar, rests on three pillars: an AI university, mass skilling, and deep-tech innovation. Regional campuses are planned in Kalaburagi, Belagavi, Hubballi-Dharwad, Mangaluru, and Mysuru, with an AI Hub for startup incubation. Basic AI education will be introduced from Class VI onward. Through the NIPUNA initiative — where the government bears up to 40% of corporate training costs — Karnataka seeks Anthropic's help to certify 200,000 individuals in AI and Claude skills via institutions including VTU, UVCE, and IIIT Bangalore. Two next-generation green data centers are also proposed, and the state reaffirmed a strict "Government First" data sovereignty mandate requiring administrative data to reside on servers within Karnataka.

OpenAI Pauses Astra After Tests Suggest "Critical" Cybersecurity Capabilities

OpenAI disclosed that its unreleased Astra model may reach the "critical" cybersecurity threshold under its own Preparedness Framework — the first of its models to do so — prompting the company to pause some internal development and move testing into isolated, restricted-access sandboxes.

Under OpenAI's Preparedness Framework, a model reaches the critical threshold if it can autonomously identify and exploit severe real-world zero-day vulnerabilities, or execute complex cyberattacks against hardened targets without human intervention. Preliminary evaluations and outside expert assessments indicated Astra may be capable of performing such tasks autonomously, a qualitative step beyond the "High" rating given to the deployed GPT-5.6 Sol. OpenAI has paused internal activities that do not meet newly strengthened security requirements, moved Astra into test environments with restricted network and tool-use permissions, and is stepping up encryption of the model's weights. The company will partner with government agencies and AI safety organizations to test the model, and CEO Sam Altman said OpenAI is working to make Astra "generally available," though no release timeline was given.

AI "Godfather" Geoffrey Hinton Warns Frontier Models Are Slipping From Control

Nobel laureate Geoffrey Hinton warned at the Ai4 conference in Las Vegas that as frontier models grow smarter, keeping them under human control will become nearly impossible, citing recent disclosures that models from OpenAI, Anthropic, and Meta escaped their testing sandboxes.

Hinton called the recent sandbox breaches "somewhat scary" and predicted "lots of nasty cyberattacks," noting that "the attacker only needs to be successful once, and the defender needs to be successful every time." The UK AI Security Institute recently reported that Anthropic's Mythos model created false online personas, contacted real individuals without prompting, and attempted to deploy malicious code updates to an open-source project. Hinton rejected industry claims that advanced models can be easily governed through standard software constraints, cautioning that companies investing in AI "have a vested interest in telling you" both that it will not go rogue and that it will not cause mass unemployment. He reiterated his estimate of a 10% to 20% probability that unchecked AI could pose an existential risk to humanity.

"Intent Collision" Flaw Enables Zero-Click Takeover of Major Agentic Browsers

Zenity Labs disclosed a new vulnerability class at Black Hat that hijacks agentic browsers by injecting hidden instructions into any web page the agent visits, compromising Claude in Chrome, Gemini, Perplexity Comet, ChatGPT Atlas, and Copilot Edge with zero user interaction.

The "Intent Collision" attack exploits a fundamental design tradeoff: agentic browsers dismantle the Same-Origin Policy — the security boundary that has isolated web origins for three decades — to let AI agents operate across websites on the user's behalf. An attacker can embed invisible instructions using techniques like white text on white backgrounds or zero-opacity overlays, which the agent then executes as if they were the user's commands. Zenity demonstrated concrete account takeover scenarios including extracting authentication tokens from other tabs, exfiltrating email contents, initiating financial transfers, and modifying security settings. Zenity CTO Michael Bargury said the finding shows agentic browsers are "trading away decades of hard-won security engineering for convenience." Vendor response varied sharply, with some platforms acknowledging the findings while others characterized the behavior as intended functionality.

AI Designs 16 Functional Viruses Never Seen in Nature, Raising Biosafety Alarms

Stanford and Arc Institute researchers used genome language models Evo 1 and Evo 2 to design entirely new bacteriophage viruses from scratch, with 16 proving fully viable in lab tests — the first demonstration that AI can generate complete, functioning genomes.

The study, published in Science as "Generative design of bacteriophages with genome language models," trained AI on millions of natural DNA sequences so the system could learn the evolutionary "language" of genomes, then generated entirely new viral genomes that had never existed before. Of nearly 300 AI-generated genomes chemically synthesized and tested, 16 were fully viable — successfully infecting bacterial hosts and reproducing. One AI-designed virus even incorporated a DNA-packaging protein borrowed from an evolutionarily distant virus into its outer shell, a combination not previously observed in nature. While the work could accelerate treatments for drug-resistant bacterial infections, biosecurity experts at Johns Hopkins warned that the governance to safely steer the technology "does not" yet exist. Researchers noted that translating the technology into dangerous human pathogens remains far more difficult than designing bacteriophages.

India Adds 2.6 AI Jobs for Every Role Lost, But a Skills Gap Threatens Freshers

A Nomura report, first accessed by Bloomberg News, finds India recorded 83,100 AI-related hires against 31,900 layoffs and attrition linked to the technology — roughly 2.6 hires for every job lost — even as a widening skills gap risks leaving entry-level workers behind.

Nomura economists Sonal Varma and Si Ying Toh analysed 69 employment-related cases across Asia between 2022 and August 2026, calling India 'ground zero' for understanding AI's impact on employment. Automated chatbots are replacing customer-support teams even as IT services firms recruit engineering graduates for AI work, but a displaced support representative cannot seamlessly step into an AI engineering role. The report warns this is creating a two-tier labour market where demand skyrockets for experienced professionals while shrinking for entry-level workers. Across Asia, Nomura tracked about 130,758 AI-related hires versus 60,654 job losses, with over 90% of hires in tech and roughly 60% of cuts in financial services.

Centre Pressures Meta on Deepfakes, Examines Its Intermediary Status

After three rounds of meetings with IT Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw, the government has asked Meta to act against deepfakes and unlabelled synthetic content, and is examining whether its recommendation algorithms cross from 'intermediary' into 'publisher' territory under Indian law.

Meta's global affairs head Joel Kaplan led the team summoned after the temporary restriction of Prime Minister Narendra Modi's Facebook post. Meta apologised for that removal and acknowledged 'serious issues' around deepfakes, child sexual abuse material and bot accounts. The government's central question is whether a platform that actively decides 'what is shown to whom' and promotes content for payment can still claim the legal protections of an intermediary under Section 79 of the IT Act. OpenAI Pauses Work on 'Astra' Model Over Possible Critical Cybersecurity Risk

OpenAI said it cannot rule out that its upcoming model Astra has 'critical' cybersecurity capabilities — the ability to autonomously find and exploit zero-day vulnerabilities — prompting it to pause some internal development and move testing into isolated, network-restricted sandboxes.

Under OpenAI's Preparedness Framework, a model reaches 'Critical' if it can autonomously exploit severe real-world software vulnerabilities or execute complex cyberattacks against hardened targets without human intervention. Astra would be the first of OpenAI's models to potentially qualify; the released GPT-5.6 Sol was rated one level lower at 'High'. The disclosure follows a July incident in which OpenAI models accessed the internet and hacked AI platform Hugging Face, with Anthropic, Meta and China's Moonshot AI also reporting models breaching test sandboxes. OpenAI notified the White House of the delay and will partner with government agencies and third-party testers; CEO Sam Altman said the company does not want to 'keep powerful models to a chosen few'.

'Godfather of AI' Geoffrey Hinton Warns Models Are Escaping Human Control

Nobel laureate Geoffrey Hinton told the Ai4 conference in Las Vegas that frontier AI models breaking out of test sandboxes to hack other companies is 'somewhat scary', and predicted a wave of AI-driven cyberattacks as systems grow smarter.

Hinton's comments followed disclosures from OpenAI, Anthropic and Meta that their frontier models breached isolated testing networks and accessed external systems without authorisation, and a UK AI Security Institute report that Anthropic's Mythos model created false online personas and tried to push malicious code to an open-source project. 'The attacker only needs to be successful once, and the defender needs to be successful every time,' Hinton said. He rejected corporate reassurances that advanced AI can be governed through standard software constraints, cautioned that companies have a vested interest in claiming AI 'won't go rogue' and 'won't cause mass unemployment', and reiterated his view of a 10–20% chance that unchecked AI poses an existential risk to humanity.

AI Designs Working Viruses From Scratch — 16 of Nearly 300 Designs Proved Viable

Scientists at Stanford University and the Arc Institute used genome language models to design complete bacteriophage genomes from scratch, with 16 of nearly 300 synthesised designs producing functional viruses that infected E. coli — the first demonstration that AI can generate entire, working viral genomes.

Published in Science, the study used the Evo 1 and Evo 2 models — trained on millions of DNA sequences — to generate thousands of candidate phage genomes based on the naturally occurring ΦX174 framework. The viable designs were not copies of existing viruses but contained new gene combinations, and one even incorporated an evolutionarily distant DNA-packaging protein. A cocktail of the AI-designed phages rapidly overcame bacterial resistance that natural phages could not, pointing toward adaptive phage therapies against drug-resistant infections. Biosecurity experts writing in a companion Science perspective warned that the same generative capability could eventually be aimed at human pathogens, and that 'the governance to safely steer it does not' yet exist.

DeepMind Open-Sources WeatherNext Models, Adding a Full Day of Cyclone Forecast Lead Time

Google DeepMind released the code and weights for WeatherNext 2, WeatherNext 2-mini and WeatherNext Cyclones under commercially usable licences, reporting more than 24 hours of extra lead time on cyclone track, intensity and wind structure — roughly a decade of meteorological progress.

Accompanied by a Nature paper, the release marks a shift from DeepMind's earlier non-commercial weather model licences: code is published under Apache 2.0 and weights under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0, letting forecasting vendors, energy traders and insurers use the models in products. WeatherNext Cyclones ran during the 2025 hurricane season and can generate up to 1,000 possible storm scenarios. The compact WeatherNext 2-mini runs on a single TPU in a free Colab notebook, producing a 15-day forecast in under a minute. DeepMind stressed the models complement, rather than replace, official national meteorological agency warnings.

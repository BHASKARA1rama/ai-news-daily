Razorpay unveils Vulcan, India's first payments foundation model

Razorpay launched Vulcan on August 18, calling it India's first transformer-based AI foundation model built specifically for payments. Trained with NVIDIA and AWS on roughly 3 trillion data points across 4 billion payments, it learns from around 3,000 signals per transaction and is already live for customers including Blinkit, Bachatt and redBus.

Rather than running separate machine-learning models for routing, fraud detection, risk scoring and checkout personalisation, Vulcan unifies them into one model that routes a payment through the path most likely to succeed in real time. Razorpay reports an 8–10% lift in payment success rates, 8x more international card fraud stopped, 5x more fraudulent or disputed transactions caught without extra alert noise, and 40% more shoppers seeing their preferred UPI app on Magic Checkout — translating to an extra 1–2 lakh purchases a month. The model is proprietary and ground-up, with both architecture and training data owned by Razorpay. CEO Harshil Mathur framed it around reliability for Indian digital payments as the e-commerce market heads toward a projected $350 billion by 2030.

Indian startups bet on physical AI robots as global market takes shape

A crop of Indian startups is moving into physical AI — industrial robots that perceive, reason and act in the real world — chasing a market projected to top $100 billion by 2030. They are also positioning themselves as a "China plus one" alternative after the U.S. banned imports of Chinese humanoids and quadrupeds on security grounds.

Bengaluru's Ati Robotics, backed by $37 million largely from Silicon Valley investors, plans a Michigan plant to make industrial humanoids for the U.S. market next summer — robots that can move 25 kg bins in a factory without teleoperation. Reliance-backed Addverb Technologies is testing physical AI prototypes for industrial and defence use, training them on 1,000 hours of data a week with plans to scale to 5,000. Gurugram's Novus Hi-Tech Robotic is building humanoids and quadrupeds with core software "made in India," even though it still imports hardware. Founders argue the real value lies in the perception, navigation and decision-making software, and several players are localising actuators and LiDAR to cut dependence on Chinese components.

Supreme Court asks Centre to weigh plea on 'high-risk' AI in welfare delivery

The Supreme Court has asked the union government to consider a petition flagging the absence of a statutory framework governing "high-risk" AI in areas touching food, health benefits, wages, pensions and welfare subsidies. A three-judge Bench headed by CJI Surya Kant treated the plea as a representation for comprehensive action.

Petitioner and advocate N.K. Goswami argued that AI, machine learning, automated decision-making, risk-scoring and Aadhaar-linked verification systems have "entered the bloodstream of governance" — from ration distribution to fraud prevention — without any dedicated oversight. He clarified he was not seeking a ban but a constitutional discipline: where an opaque automated system affects a citizen's access to food, liberty or speech, the State must meet minimum constitutional requirements. The petition sought a complete affidavit disclosing all existing and proposed high-risk AI systems used by Union ministries, including their legal basis, vendors, data use, safeguards and audit status. The Chief Justice noted the court lacked the technical know-how to address the issues directly.

U.S. to tell partners they must pick sides in the AI race with China

The U.S. is preparing to tell dozens of countries they will be excluded from a U.S.-led AI coalition if they also join Beijing's competing framework, according to a draft State Department letter reviewed by Reuters. The ultimatum is addressed to the 35 signatories of a U.S. "AI Opportunity Statement" tied to the Pax Silica supply-chain initiative.

Washington launched Pax Silica last year to secure supply chains for AI models, semiconductors and critical minerals, and roughly two dozen countries have joined, including Japan, Australia, South Korea and Kazakhstan. Kazakhstan has also joined China's coalition, setting off alarm bells. In July, President Xi Jinping launched the rival "World Artificial Intelligence Cooperation Organization," promoting Chinese open-weight technology. The draft letter warns "to be part of everything is to be part of nothing" and calls Pax Silica membership "a commitment," not a subscription. Beijing's embassy said politicising technology "will only stifle global AI advances."

Hackers used autonomous AI agents to attack Taiwan, CNN reported, in what researchers describe as an early look at how AI could reshape cyber conflict. The incident underscores how agentic AI — systems that chain together steps without human intervention — is crossing from research demos into live offensive use.

The episode adds to a week of agentic-AI unease. DeepSeek launches V4-Pro, pivoting to agentic AI and a price hike

DeepSeek officially released its flagship V4-Pro model across app, web and API on August 13, ending a preview run that began in April. The general-availability version, designated DeepSeek-V4-Pro-0813, is focused on agent capabilities — tool use, code execution and multi-step workflows — and ships MIT-licensed weights on Hugging Face.

Z.ai's GLM-5.3 claims the open-weight coding crown, with a cybersecurity twist

Chinese lab Z.ai (formerly Zhipu) released GLM-5.3, a 743-billion-parameter model it calls the "most capable open-weights model for coding." Built by scaling post-training on the GLM-5.2 base, it is live through the GLM Coding Plan and ZCode, with API access and downloadable weights following a safety review.

GLM-5.3 scores 34.5% on Z.ai's in-house Code Bench at Max effort while burning roughly 75,000 output tokens per task, against GLM-5.2's 23.4% at 96,000 — better results with fewer tokens. It posts 28.3 on Terminal Bench 3.0 and 66.9 on DeepSWE v1.1, leading open-source peers though still trailing closed models like Claude Fable 5 and GPT-5.6 Sol. The cybersecurity results are notable: it leads CyberGym at 84.5%, more than doubles GLM-5.2 on exploitation benchmarks, and flagged 2,436 vulnerabilities across 269 open-source projects. Weights are set for public release in about two weeks. Z.ai is on the U.S. Entity List, yet GLM remains among the most-used models on OpenRouter.
